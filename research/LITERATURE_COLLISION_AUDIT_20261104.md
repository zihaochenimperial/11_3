## P1817

- [Luxar's pinned tutorial](https://github.com/royerlab/luxar/blob/318eee294ac9909cf27ca12670022455bb7b5de8/docs/tutorials/gaussian_splatting.rst#L315) already calibrates K by blind-spot loss and can use an auto-selected region, transferring calibrated density into content-based per-box budgets. The earlier description "global K only" was too broad and is withdrawn.
- [Image-GS](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.01866v2) and [GaussianImage++](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.19108v2) already add Gaussians in under-reconstructed regions using training-target residual/distortion. [SAD](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.21984v2) also describes residual splitting and pruning. Local adaptation or pruning alone is occupied territory.
- Narrow candidate: a precisely specified, leakage-safe held-out **accept/reject decision on individual sequential local edits** in a 2D Gaussian representation, with improvement beyond regional blind-spot budget calibration and residual birth. The [Noise2Self identity](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/batson19a.html) is existing theory; repeated use of B for adaptive proposals invalidates the simple fixed-candidate argument. The source search has not established priority or an improvement.

## P1901

- [Cool-Chic 5.0](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.02726v2) already studies encoding-time/quality tradeoffs. Its [pinned training code](https://github.com/Orange-OpenSource/Cool-Chic/blob/a6fe38a414dd098b39c41636bd6e423626402f7e/coolchic/training/train.py#L183) has a patience mechanism; per-image early stopping cannot be claimed as new. [Reduced-complexity coding](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.11651v1) and [HyperCool](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.18748v1) already address faster encoding. [MoRIC's official repository](https://github.com/eedavidwu/MoRIC) reports actual bitstream support, though its full paper was not verified in this pass.
- Narrow candidate: an online rule that allocates one enforced **total wall-clock budget across multiple complete image encodes**, counting exploration, quantization, and bitstream work, and improving pixel-weighted decoded rate-distortion over simple allocations. Marginal-gain equalization is established conditional mathematics, not a novel algorithm. It is worth testing only if a discrete oracle on multiple images shows headroom greater than run variation.
